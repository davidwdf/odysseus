Place detail — round 3: timeline, then saved, then grouped by kerb

“I think we could merge the existing place design with the timeline? So the timeline at the top shows the buses arriving soon, while below that is the list of favourites at that stop, and then below that is the structured list where they are grouped by stop ID? Would that be a good balance?”

Yes — and it is a better home for the timeline than Home was. Two reasons it fits here and did not fit there. First, the density is bounded: one place, not an aggregate, so the interchange case that killed the uniform axis is the worst case rather than the normal one. Second — and this is the argument that makes it earn its space rather than decorate — the timeline is the only view on this screen that ignores kerb boundaries. The grouped list exists precisely to respect them; a rider standing at a merged place who just wants the next bus has, until now, had to read three kerbs and do the merge in their head.

Press “Next round” to land fresh data: the axis re-lays out, and any bus that actually changed places in the order is outlined. Compare against the shipping screen on the left.

Is it a good balance? Mostly yes — with one risk worth your eyes

Each zone answers a different question, which is the test the previous two rounds failed. The timeline: what is leaving this place soon, from any kerb? The saved block: what about the routes I actually take? The grouped list: which kerb do I stand at, and what else goes from it? None is a restatement of another, and the screen reads top-to-bottom from “glance” to “decide” to “look up”.

⚠️ The risk: one bus can appear three times. A saved route leaving in four minutes is a token on the axis, a row in your saved block, and a row under its kerb. On the busy place with two saved routes that is six duplicated lines out of about twenty. Three mitigations, in order of how much I believe in them:

  • Keep the timeline short. Ten minutes, not thirty — so it holds four or five tokens and reads as a headline rather than a second list. That is what the mockup does.
  • Put the kerb code on the timeline token (the small grey D1 / D2 badge). It stops the axis being a duplicate because it carries the one fact the grouped list is organised around — the rider can go straight from the token to the right kerb.
  • Do not dim or hide duplicates. Tempting, and it is the silent-filter trap: ADR-008's rule is that hiding an honest row because it appears elsewhere costs a rider more than the repetition does.

The quiet-place case is the one that decides whether this is structural. Switch to Man Ming Lane: one kerb, three routes. The grouped list collapses to a flat list (as it already does — PlaceDetailView.grouped is false), the timeline holds three tokens, and the screen is arguably all headline. If that reads as empty ceremony, the timeline wants a floor — “only when this place has more than N departures, or more than one kerb”. That would be a kernel rule, not a view's.

What this round is asking

  • Is the three-zone stack the right balance, or is one zone too many?
  • Does the kerb badge on the timeline token earn its place? It is what stops the axis being a duplicate.
  • Quiet place: keep the timeline, or gate it behind a floor?
