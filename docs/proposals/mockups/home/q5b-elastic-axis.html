Home — Q5 round 2: an elastic axis, and what happens when the data moves

“This is actually pretty cool. How would we handle shifting positions (bus arrivals are estimates and the order could change)? I agree it does make it a little harder to track specific bus routes. The timeline can be flexible right? — e.g. we adjust the spacing between minutes if there's a lot of buses crowding. I also like the clean mixed integration of minibus stops with the main stops. The ±2 min band is no good really — bus info is an estimate and on a busy route the overlap only creates confusion.”

Three answers, built. The ± band is gone — you are right, and it was the wrong shape of honesty: it drew our uncertainty as a smear rather than saying anything. The elastic axis is the middle phone, and it works better than I expected. Shifting positions is the “Next round” button — press it and a fresh round lands: times change, two buses swap order, one departs.

The rule that makes shifting safe, and it is one we already have. ADR-008 forbids a client-side per-second countdown — the app updates a figure only when fresh data arrives. So the axis never slides under a fixed “now”; it is a snapshot that re-lays out when a round lands, exactly as the list's numerals re-render. The axis inherits the honesty rule the list already lives under and adds no new exposure. What it adds is that a change is now visible — a numeral going 11 → 8 is easy to miss, a bus moving is not.

Round 1 — rejected

Uniform axis

Position is a linear function of minutes. Honest at a quiet stop, and it collapses on a busy one: tokens collide, the nudge (amber) fires, and every nudged token is drawn at a time its bus does not depart.

Your suggestion

Elastic axis

Crowded stretches of the timeline are given more room and empty ones are compressed — and the ruler stretches with them. That last part is what keeps it honest: position still equals time as read against the ticks, because the ticks moved too. A stretched interval draws its rule dashed, so the axis says where it gave.

My lean

Hybrid — axis as headline, list as reference

A short elastic axis of the next few minutes sits above the full list. The axis answers “what is the shape of the next ten minutes”; the list answers “when is my 73X”. Neither is asked to do the other's job, which is where both previous rounds broke.

Your four points, answered

1. Shifting positions. The axis re-lays out only when a round lands — never between rounds, because a token creeping toward “now” is the per-second countdown ADR-008 bans, just drawn instead of typed. When a round does land, every token animates to its new place over one motion (600 ms, the sheet's own easing), a bus that has departed slides off the top and fades — that is useRailFlip's existing exit, reused — and a bus that has changed order is outlined in green for that one transition. Press Next round and watch rows 2 and 3 swap.
⚠️ The thing to judge: whether reordering reads as the board being alive or as the board being unreliable. The list has the same reorder and hides it — which is either kinder or less honest, and that is a taste question I cannot settle for you.

2. Harder to track a specific route. Real, and the hybrid is the answer rather than a fix: the list underneath is sorted by time but scannable by chip, so “where is my 73X” stays a lookup. An axis alone will always be worse at this, because its sort key is the thing you are not searching by.

3. A flexible timeline. Built, and it is the best idea in this round. The rule: find the gaps between consecutive departures, give every interval a minimum height so a clump can breathe, and move the ruler's ticks by the same mapping. Draw a stretched interval's rule dashed. The nudge count goes to zero at every density in the mockup — the axis stops lying because it no longer has to.
The honest cost: the axis is no longer uniformly readable. Two tokens a centimetre apart are three minutes apart in one stretch and one minute apart in another, so the shape of bunching — the thing the axis existed to show — is flattened by the very mechanism that makes it fit. Compare the busy stop on the middle phone against the left one and you can see the trade directly.

4. The ± band. Removed. Your reasoning is better than my defence of it: drawing uncertainty as an overlapping smear on a busy board adds confusion without adding information, and the app's existing answer to “this is an estimate” is the word approximately in the copy and a board-level freshness line — not a per-figure cue. That is ADR-123's conclusion, twice-decided; the band was me proposing it a third time in a new costume.

What this round is asking

  • Uniform, elastic, or hybrid? My lean is hybrid-with-an-elastic-axis, which is the third phone.
  • Press “Next round” three or four times on the busy stop. Does the movement read as alive or as jittery? That single judgement decides whether any of this ships.
  • In the hybrid, is the seam right? The axis is on a tinted panel to say “this is a summary, not the list”. It could equally be plain, or collapsible.
