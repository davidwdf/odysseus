Home — Q5: is a time axis honest, and do you want one?

Same six-to-twenty departures, two renderings. Left is what the app ships today — one row per route, the arrival as a figure on the right. Right is the time axis — a ruler down the side, each departure drawn as a bus token at its position, the way the route rail draws a bus at its stop.

The question is ADR-008. We never fake precision, and a position on a ruler is a continuous claim built from a value we insist is approximate. It could go either way — a token sitting between the 10 and the 15 arguably says "about then" better than 11 min does. Change the stop and the window below; Hong Kong frequency is the thing most likely to kill this.

What the axis is actually trading

What it buys. Bunching and gaps become shapes rather than arithmetic you do in your head. Three buses at 11, 12 and 13 minutes is a fact the list states three times and the axis states once. And a token between two marks reads as "about then", which is what an ETA actually is.

What it costs — the honest list.

  • Density. Try the interchange at 10 minutes. The tokens collide, the nudge kicks in (outlined in amber), and every nudged token is now drawn at a time it does not depart. That is the axis lying, and it is caused by the very crowding it was supposed to illustrate.
  • Vertical budget. The list fits ~9 departures in a phone's worth of sheet. The axis fits the window you give it and no more — 30 minutes of Mong Kok does not fit in any sheet.
  • Scanning. "When is my 73X" is a lookup. A list sorted by route answers it; an axis sorted by time makes you search.
  • It cannot show "—". A route with no reading has no position. The list says — in a column; the axis has to put it somewhere or drop it, and dropping it is the silent filter ADR-008 forbids.

The ± band toggle is the obvious answer to the precision worry: draw the uncertainty rather than a point. It works, and it costs the density you had left.

The verdict this is asking you for

  • Yes — build it for quiet stops only, and fall back to the list above some density. (Honest, but two renderings of one board is two things to keep in sync, and the rider does not control which they get.)
  • Yes — build it as the window, not the board: a short axis above the list showing the next 10 minutes, with the full list underneath. The axis becomes a summary rather than a replacement.
  • No — drop it. The list is honest, dense, scannable, and already shipping.

My read after building it: the second option is the only one that survives Mong Kok. A 10-minute axis is a headline — "here is the shape of the next ten minutes" — and a list is a reference. They answer different questions, and the mockup's density problem only bites when you ask the axis to be the reference. But this is exactly the call worth making with your own eyes rather than from my paragraph.
