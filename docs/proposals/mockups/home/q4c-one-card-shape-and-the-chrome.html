You are right about the star, and it was a real error in round 2. A favourite is formatFavoriteRouteKey(stopId, routeId) — a route at a pole. A place is never starred, so round 2's star on the card heading was claiming something the data model cannot express. It is on the route now.

Your second suggestion collapses the thing I was worried about. Round 2's open risk was “two row shapes on one screen — does the seam read as deliberate?” Putting the chip strip on saved places too means there is only one card shape: a heading, then any routes you starred here with times, then everything else at this place as chips. A nearby card is simply that card with no starred rows yet. The seam is gone because the two halves are now the same object.

The expanding strip is built. +14 is a tappable badge; expanded, every route wraps and a chevron-up ends the list. Tap any strip to try it.

Wrapping the badges — feasible, with one rule to get right

Yes, and it is cheap. The chips are a wrapping flex row; expanded, a 26-route interchange is four or five lines of chips, which is less height than five route rows would have been. The +14 badge and the chevron-up are both real controls in the strip's flow, so nothing floats and nothing overlaps.

The rule to get right: how many chips show while collapsed must be the kernel's number, not the view's. check-no-derivation bans a renderer doing arithmetic over rows, and “show the first N and count the rest” is exactly that — it is the same shape as the +N more routes cap stopCardView already owns. So homeView hands over the full ordered chip list and the collapsed count, the view renders expanded ? all : first(collapsed), and the +N label comes from the catalogue with the kernel's number in it. Expanding is a rider's tap — interaction, not a domain rule — so that part is legitimately the view's.

One thing to decide with your eyes: whether the collapsed strip should be one line or two. One line is tidier; two shows most of a normal stop without any tap at all, and only a real interchange keeps a +N. The mockup shows two.

The tab bar — your question, and I think the answer is no

The bar has run out of destinations. Walk the three candidates from §8 of the proposal against this design: Saved is now a section of Home, so a tab for it is a second door to something already on screen. Map is the sheet's lowest detent — drag down and Home is the map view, which is better than a tab because it keeps your board one gesture away instead of one navigation. Home is the only root left, and a one-tab bar is not a bar.

So: two floating lenses and no bar. Settings top-right, Search bottom-right, matching the back lens's geometry that already ships. That is phone B, and it is my lean.

⚠️ Three things it costs, stated plainly. (1) Discoverability — two unlabelled circles replace three labelled tabs, and Search is the app's second-most-used surface. Phone C tries the obvious mitigation: a wide, labelled search pill instead of a lens. (2) A future home for ferries, rail or a full-city map — the backlog's own reason to keep a bar. Adding a bar back later is a bigger change than keeping one. (3) ⚠️ It amends ADR-100: the floating glass tab bar is identity, ported value-for-value at your direction after the parity review. Removing it is your call to make, but it is a call, not a layout tweak — and it wants an ADR that says the bar was retired because its destinations dissolved, not because the chrome was disliked.

What I would actually do: ship phone C. The labelled pill costs nothing, answers the discoverability objection, and leaves the bottom edge free for a bar to come back to if ferries ever land.
