Home — round 2: what is this screen for?

“I much prefer the new tabbar shown in these designs (having it centered is nice and will work better on larger screens). To be honest, I struggle with this design, because I'm not 100% clear on what I'm supposed to be doing on this screen. I guess that can be where lists really shine — browsable. Hmm… what if we only showed nearby stops and not buses specifically? That seems like it could hurt the usability a bit, but be a little easier — find the “stop/place” nearest you.”

That is the more useful question, and round 1 was asking the wrong one. Which words go in the floating card cannot be answered while the board underneath is unclear — so the card is held constant across all three phones below (it says where you are), and what changes is the board. The card question comes back once this one is settled.

The diagnosis, stated plainly: round 1's Home asked a rider to do triage. Three sections, each stop showing two or three of its routes with times, plus a map, plus a cap of +N more hiding the rest. Route detail is clear because it answers one question — where is this bus and when does it get to my stop. Round 1's Home answered three at once and ranked them.

Why C is the lean, in one paragraph

Your two occasions from Q2 want different things, and the screen has been trying to serve both with one row shape. “Which of my routes is next” and “when do I leave the office” are checking — you already know the route, so a time is the whole answer and we can be specific because you told us which ones matter. “What's around me” is browsing — we do not know which of a stop's 26 routes you want, so picking three and hiding 23 behind +23 more is a guess dressed as an answer.

So: saved content gets times; discovery content gets stops. One job per section, and each section's row shape says which job it is doing. That is what makes the screen legible — not a better ranking, and not a better card.

It also deletes a standing problem. +N more routes — which docs/07 has an open question about, because from Favourites it dumps you into an unfiltered list of 20 — stops existing in the discovery section, because nothing is capped when nothing claims to be a shortlist.

What each option costs, honestly

  • A (round 1) — most information, least clarity. Every section looks the same, so nothing tells you which rows are yours and which are the algorithm's. It is the one you struggled with, and the sameness is why.
  • B (yours: stops only) — the cleanest thing on this page, and the map finally means something: one row, one pin. But Home stops answering the question that makes this app worth opening. "Is my bus coming" now takes a tap, and the "before leaving the office" case takes a tap into a screen about a place you are not at.
  • C (the split) — clear at a glance which half is yours. Cost: two row shapes on one screen, which is the thing to judge with your eyes. If it reads as two unrelated lists stapled together, C fails and B is the honest fallback.

What this round is asking

  • A, B or C — and if C, does the seam between the two halves read as deliberate or as a glitch?
  • In B and C's "Around you", is the chip strip right? It is there so you can spot your route number without us claiming a time for it. The alternative is the bare stop name and a count ("26 routes"), which is cleaner still and makes you tap to find out if it is even the right stop.
  • Try "Nothing saved yet". That is a cold install, and it is the state that decides whether the split is a structure or a special case — in C, a new rider sees B.

Settled from your note and not being re-asked: the tab bar is centred, with the search lens floating separately at the bottom right. That is what all three phones draw.
