Round 4 — your proposal: one context card that shrinks by width

The header is a floating card that tucks behind the back lens when open, with a large centred badge and fare/frequency/schedule inside it. Any map drag or list scroll collapses it — the card steps right to clear the back lens, its content shifts left, the badge shrinks, and the body folds away, leaving a full-width pill that gives the destination as much room as possible. Tap it to expand again. Split starts 50/50. Tapping a stop opens the action sheet, as today.

Layers on screen

Collapsed = full width, clear of the back lens Expanded, the card runs the full width and tucks behind the back lens; the badge is large and centred, which pushes the destination clear of the button. Collapsed, the card's left edge moves right to clear the lens, its content shifts left, and the badge shrinks — so the pill is still full width and gives the destination as much room as it can.

Why the width-collapse beats the badge-morph I built in round 3 Collapsing by width keeps the badge and the destination in one row that never re-flows — so it is a single CSS/layout transition, which both renderers can express identically. The badge-morph in round 3 needed interpolated position and scale and two cross-fading labels, which is exactly why apps/web's version drifted from apps/mobile's.
  • ✓ Fewer moving parts, so parity is cheap rather than hard-won.
  • ✓ Binary state + tap-to-expand is predictable: it never re-expands under your thumb.
  • ✗ It no longer re-expands when you scroll back to the top — see the toggle.
