Round 4 — your proposal: one context card that shrinks by width

The header is a floating card that tucks behind the back lens when open, with a large centred badge and fare/frequency/schedule inside it. Any map drag or list scroll collapses it — the card steps right to clear the back lens, its content shifts left, the badge shrinks, and the body folds away, leaving a full-width pill that gives the destination as much room as possible. Tap it to expand again. Split starts 50/50. Tapping a stop opens the action sheet, as today.

Path & marker styling

Colours are real tokens, not hand-picked hex. Current zoom: —

Line

Neutral dark is #33322F — a warm grey, and it is NOT our ink. BRAND.ink is #111827 and is declared "theme-independent … does not invert with the appearance", which is exactly what a route line cannot be. Every colour here now carries a light/dark pair: toggle Dark map and the line goes light, marker fills go dark, their borders and the hairline go light, and the chevrons stay in the casing colour so they darken with it. The tiles invert by filter; the overlay inverts by design.

Line colour in dark mode

Turn on Dark map below to compare. Two things the inversion does, both of which decide this: LandsD's black label text becomes white, so a near-white line ("Paper") reads as one more label rather than as the route; and the yellow road fills become warm tan, so Amber collides with the road network — the same mistake CTB's yellow makes on the light map, in the opposite direction. Cyan and Mint sit furthest from both, which is why cyan is the default. Markers, hairlines and chevrons keep their inverted pair regardless.

Direction marks
Gap between the two chevrons
Avoid bends

A mark that straddles a corner points along neither leg of it. Each mark now measures the heading change across its own footprint and slides along its slot to the straightest spot it can reach; if even the best position is still a corner, nothing is drawn there — a missing mark costs nothing, a misleading one does.

The pair is now one glyph: a single rotated group placed at the mark's centre, with the two chevrons offset inside that group's own frame. Previously each was placed at its own arc position and rotated by the tangent there, so on a bend the two splayed apart and stopped reading as one mark.

Marks are placed between consecutive stops — one for a short hop, two for a long one — rather than at a fixed interval. The stops are the rhythm a rider already reads, so the direction marks follow it instead of landing on top of them.

Markers & map
Marker position

You were right: Hong Kong drives on the left and riders board on the left, so every stop should sit on the left of its direction of travel. Measured on KMB 1: of the 19 stops whose coordinate is offset from the line at all, 19 are on the left and 0 on the right (the other 6 sit within 1.5 px of it). So Left of travel and Side the data says agree here — the forced version is still the safer default, because one noisy coordinate cannot flip a marker onto the wrong kerb.

Label size

LandsD bakes label size into the raster, so the only way to get bigger text is to draw a lower zoom level's labels scaled up: bigger and sparser, but softer. A vector basemap is the only way to have both.
